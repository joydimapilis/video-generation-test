Model and tool routing

Do not force one model to create the whole video.

For every shot, determine which tool or model is best suited for the task.

Use:

* HyperFrames for deterministic UI, text-heavy scenes, motion graphics, Slack-style interfaces, and layouts where text accuracy matters.
* Video-generation models for cinematic motion, people, camera movement, or scenes where generative video adds value.
* Image-to-video as the preferred workflow for shots involving people when possible.

Choose the model based on the shot requirements and the strengths learned from our previous tests.

People / image-to-video workflow

For any shot involving a person, do not default directly to text-to-video.

Use this process:

1. Review the video library and identify the best inspiration for:
    * pose
    * framing
    * camera angle
    * lighting
    * clothing
    * environment
    * gesture
    * intended movement
2. Generate a high-quality reference image of the person first.
3. Review the image before generating the video.

Check for:

* realistic facial features
* realistic skin texture
* natural hands and fingers
* correct body posture
* correct objects in the person’s hands
* natural clothing
* correct environment
* believable pose
* good eye direction
* no obvious AI artifacts

4. Once the image is acceptable, use image-to-video to animate the shot.
5. Choose the strongest available image-to-video model based on the action required.
6. Pay special attention to:

* eye direction and gaze
* natural hand movement
* realistic body movement
* object interaction
* facial micro-expressions
* realistic skin texture
* hair and clothing movement
* lip sync when applicable
* consistent identity between shots
* avoiding overly polished or obviously AI-generated faces

7. If the motion looks unnatural, do not automatically keep regenerating from the same bad source image.

Determine whether the problem comes from:

* the source image
* the prompt
* the model
* the camera instruction
* the pose
* the action

Then either improve the source image, rewrite the prompt, or route the shot to a different model.

Screen-led product demo: “Ask, plan, review”

Testable angle: Kite makes the operating loop visible in the place the team already works, turning one marketing request into a plan and reviewable work.

Audience tension: Buyers do not need another place to manage. They need to see what will happen after they hand off a marketing job and where their approval fits.

Format and duration: 42 seconds. Screen-first walkthrough with minimal voiceover and precise on-screen captions.

“A launch needs more than a caption. It needs the work around it to move.”

On-screen:
One request. The work around it.

On-screen:
