## Creative interpretation

This video tells buyers with too much marketing work that Kite makes the work visible inside Slack, prepares drafts, and leaves the public-change decision with them in the depicted Propose first workflow.

Open immediately on “One request. The work around it.” Four labelled launch cards accumulate over three seconds, then collapse into the same conversation surface that carries the rest of the film. Make the complexity visible with four concrete deliverables, without adding urgency claims or statistics. Keep the thread anchored through request, plan, research, drafts and approval. Interest comes from changing the work state, restrained orange emphasis and the four-to-one card transformation. No scene requires a person or a generated video plate.

Use the official Kite wordmark, white canvas, near-black text, restrained newsroom orange, and captured Onest typography. The newsroom palette differs from some current homepage UI blues; orange is the proposed campaign accent, subject to brand-owner confirmation. Slack chrome stays minimal: workspace label, channel name, author and message blocks. No invented customer sidebar, avatars, notifications or analytics.

Transitions preserve the conversation card's position. A 0.35–0.5s surface reflow replaces cuts between UI states; a single gentle 1.06× focus shift may introduce the plan. No repeated camera moves. Text never stretches with its container. Important screens settle before the next change. The last five seconds hold the official Kite lockup, “AI marketer in Slack”, and “Add Kite to Slack”. No URL is burned in until the campaign destination is confirmed.

Keep required content in x=108–972 and y=690–1230 on the 1080×1920 master. These coordinates fit both centered square (y=420–1500) and centered landscape (y=656.25–1263.75) crops. Open whitespace above and below is intentional; only decorative surfaces may extend beyond that band. Caption cues sit below the UI within it. Review page toggles show actual crop windows, not differently arranged layouts.
