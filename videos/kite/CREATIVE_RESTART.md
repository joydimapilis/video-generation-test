# Kite — three new creative directions

Concept proposals, 2026-09-17. Based on the new attachment `YysiDk/pasted_text_2026-09-17_13-17-56.txt`. The previous brief is background only for this new direction. These are narrative options and rough timing envelopes, not a storyboard or approved script. No new production assets or video generated.

## 1. Tomorrow

**Premise:** The product is ready. Its launch page is still empty. A marketer hands Kite the release notes and gets a page they can meaningfully review.

**Hook / opening:** “The product’s ready. The page isn’t.” A brief, quiet desk moment establishes the person; the empty launch document and “Launch tomorrow” calendar entry establish the problem. Deadline is fictional scenario context, not a promise of Kite's turnaround time.

**Scenario:** A fictional shared-inbox company is launching a team inbox. The marketer has product notes but has not turned them into a launch page. Kite gets one job: draft that page from those notes.

**Rough 38-second structure:**

- 0–7s: person, impending launch, empty page. Short cuts and restrained office sound.
- 7–14s: Slack handoff: “Kite, turn these release notes into our launch page.” Kite appears inside the existing work context.
- 14–26s: product notes become a concrete page draft. Keep one recognizable feature and its screenshot consistent across the transformation. Show the actual result at readable scale; omit a plan recital and task dashboard.
- 26–33s: the marketer reviews the page and requests, “Lead with shared ownership.” The revised hero gives the review an observable consequence.
- 33–38s: hold the revised draft, then Kite and proposed CTA “Add Kite to Slack.”

**Closing:** “Now we have something to work with.” Opening and closing provide almost all narration; UI carries the action.

**Visual character:** warm, natural desk photography followed by crisp, large product views. Pace moves from stuck to purposeful and finally settles on the work. An empty rectangle becoming a useful page is the recurring image. No floating collection of feature cards.

**Why it works:** immediately recognizable launch pressure, one concrete job, visible improvement, and human judgment that changes the output. The payoff is an editable draft, never a purportedly live website.

**Library influences:** Pocket's desk/product cutaways and ordinary daylight; Notion Custom Agents' accumulation of recognizable work; Bloom's transition from an input to tangible branded output. Borrow the narrative principles, not the conveyor, purple flowers, or human identities.

## 2. That's More Like It

**Premise:** A marketer knows the product but cannot get its launch message to sound right. One sentence becomes the visual protagonist.

**Hook / opening:** “Why is this so hard to say?” The marketer's existing draft reads “An innovative solution for modern teams.” A brief side-profile reaction establishes who is trying to solve it. The weak opening copy belongs to the fictional marketer, not a claimed Kite output.

**Scenario:** The marketer asks Kite to turn feature notes and rough copy into a clearer launch page for a fictional shared inbox. The film concentrates on the page's main promise rather than presenting a copywriting feature menu.

**Rough 34-second structure:**

- 0–6s: an overcomplicated sentence fills the frame; a restrained human pause gives the typography its emotional context.
- 6–13s: pull back into the work context; Slack request: “Rewrite this page. Start with what the customer can do.”
- 13–23s: Kite's page draft replaces abstractions with concrete copy. Main line: “One inbox for your whole team.” The surrounding page also develops, making clear this is a usable deliverable.
- 23–29s: human feedback: “Make the next step clearer.” The draft's customer-facing CTA changes from “Learn more” to “See the shared inbox.” These are fictional page-copy choices, not Kite controls.
- 29–34s: let the revised page breathe, then Kite and proposed “Add Kite to Slack.”

**Closing:** “That's more like it.”

**Visual character:** dry humor, bold editorial typography, tight crops, one consistent text anchor. Music briefly drops away around the human correction. The central sentence moves between editorial emphasis and its actual location in the page; these views should remain distinguishable from native UI.

**Why it works:** the audience can judge the improvement themselves. Human review becomes the satisfying creative moment, with a specific change rather than a ceremonial approval click.

**Library influences:** Bloom's confident typography and input-to-output structure; Typeless's visible revisions inside the same document; Pocket's grounded human inserts. Typeless's long feature-by-feature tour is deliberately not the pacing model.

**Reliability:** simplest option. One brief, near-still human insert; all important action is precise text and UI animation. No lip sync, complex hand action, or multi-shot acting continuity.

## 3. You Take the Call

**Premise:** The marketer stays present with a customer while Kite prepares the follow-up work. Two activities share the frame and converge at review.

**Hook / opening:** “Some work needs you.” A marketer listens attentively on a customer call. No audible dialogue or visible second participant is required.

**Scenario:** The marketer has handed Kite notes from a previous webinar and asked for its attendee follow-up email. While the marketer handles a separate customer conversation, Kite develops that draft. We do not imply that Kite records or listens to the live call.

**Rough 40-second structure:**

- 0–8s: human listening moment; a concise Slack handoff establishes Kite's separate assignment: “Draft the webinar follow-up from these notes. Include the recording link.”
- 8–24s: an asymmetric split composition alternates emphasis between the person and the developing email. Subject, recap, and supplied recording link provide visible substance. Editorial cuts compress elapsed time without a stopwatch or speed claim.
- 24–34s: the call ends; product view expands as the marketer reviews the email. “Put the recording link first.” The revision makes the human decision visible.
- 34–40s: revised email remains a draft; return briefly to the person, then Kite and proposed “Add Kite to Slack.”

**Closing:** “Give Kite the follow-up.” Could also work with these opening/closing lines as on-screen copy only, leaving the rest to restrained sound and music.

**Visual character:** calm, observational, warm side light. UI stays straight and readable. The human occupies a real room rather than a floating portrait bubble. A changing division of screen space expresses attention moving between customer and deliverable.

**Why it works:** delegation becomes visible as two useful activities happening alongside each other. The person has something meaningful to do beyond watching an agent produce text.

**Library influences:** Instinct's consistent product position across changing contexts, Pocket's human/product combination, and Revnu's clean reduction to one brand-and-destination ending. Avoid their public-post outcomes, talking heads, and sweeping automation claims.

**Reliability:** more demanding than the other options. Attentive listening and a believable end-of-call transition need convincing motion and identity continuity. Keep hands supported and avoid headset handling.

## Recommendation and production constraints

Choose **Tomorrow** for the clearest launch ad: immediate stakes, a substantial deliverable, a visible human decision. Choose **That's More Like It** if reliable execution with the existing system is the deciding factor. **You Take the Call** offers the most distinctive human/product composition, at greater production risk.

If selected, exact UI, text, draft revisions, and transitions belong in HyperFrames. Every human shot must begin with a generated source image reviewed for pose, hands, gaze, objects, realism, lighting, and composition before I2V. Select and test the I2V model against the actual action; reroute or regenerate weak results. Our Portion trials support keeping motion modest and inspecting gaze throughout the clip, not assuming a correct still guarantees a correct video. They are not a universal model ranking.

All concept copy is newly proposed. Example companies and work are fictional. Current approval documentation, rechecked 2026-09-17, supports requesting revisions in the thread and separately instructing publication. It does not establish current signed-in screen fidelity. Before production, verify the actual UI for the selected output type and the campaign CTA destination. Do not invent status badges, treat initiative approval as publish approval, or depict a public send/publish result. Preserve the user's review requirement through the narrative and correct product behavior.
