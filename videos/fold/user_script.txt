Create another completely new video from scratch using our existing video library, previous model learnings, scorecard, prompt/skill library, and transition learnings.

This time, create a product launch video.

The video should introduce a software, technology, digital product, or AI product and give the viewer a glimpse of how it works through a short walkthrough.

Do not make it feel like a full tutorial or long product demo. The walkthrough should only show enough of the product to make the viewer understand what it does and become interested in seeing more.

Creative direction

Make this video feel different from the previous samples while still taking inspiration from the styles, pacing, transitions, and visual ideas found in our existing video library.

Do not copy one specific reference video.

Instead, combine useful patterns from multiple videos and create a fresh concept.

Aim for something that feels like a polished modern product launch:

* Creative
* Fast-paced but easy to follow
* Visually clean
* Product-focused
* Technology-forward
* Clear in its message
* Memorable

Avoid relying heavily on interviews or people speaking directly to camera.

People can appear when useful, but the product and its experience should be the main focus.

Opening

Put extra thought into the first few seconds.

The opening should not feel generic.

Avoid immediately starting with a basic logo reveal, generic product screen, or predictable “Introducing…” text unless there is a creative reason for it.

Create a strong visual hook that makes the viewer want to keep watching.

The opening could use:

* A problem or frustrating moment
* An unexpected visual
* A bold statement
* A fast transformation
* A creative UI reveal
* A cinematic product moment
* A visual transition from the real world into the product
* Another approach inspired by patterns found in the video library

Choose the strongest idea yourself.

The opening should naturally lead into the product rather than feeling disconnected from the rest of the video.

Product reveal and walkthrough

After the hook, clearly introduce the product.

The viewer should quickly understand:

* What the product is
* What it helps with
* Why it is useful
* What makes it interesting or different

Then show a short glimpse of the product walkthrough.

Focus on a few important interactions rather than showing every feature.

For example:

problem → product reveal → key action → result

or

hook → product interface → 2–3 quick features → outcome

Use Hyperframes where appropriate for accurate UI, product screens, text, and interface animations.

Combine Hyperframes with video-generation models where cinematic scenes, environments, people, or product interactions would improve the story.

The walkthrough should feel integrated into the launch video rather than like a screen recording placed in the middle.

Product clarity

Make sure the product is clearly mentioned, shown, and understood.

Do not make the product or its purpose too subtle.

The viewer should not finish the video wondering:

“What does this product actually do?”

Keep the explanation concise, but make the value obvious.

Transitions and continuity

Continue improving transitions between every scene.

Pay particular attention to:

* Entry frames
* Exit frames
* Camera direction
* UI position
* Motion direction
* Color and lighting
* Object placement
* Scale
* Timing
* Visual rhythm

Think about how each shot enters the next shot before generating it.

Where useful, use the final frame or composition of one shot to influence the starting frame of the next.

Look for creative transitions between:

* Real-world scenes and UI
* Product screens and cinematic scenes
* Different features
* Problem and solution
* Opening and product reveal

Do not rely only on simple cuts or fades.

Ending

Give the ending the same level of attention as the opening.

Do not let the video simply stop after the walkthrough.

Create an intentional final moment that makes the video feel complete.

The ending could:

* Return to an idea introduced in the opening
* Reveal the final benefit
* Show the result of using the product
* Transition from UI back into the real world
* Use a strong final product visual
* End with a memorable CTA or concise product statement
* Visually complete a transformation started at the beginning

Where possible, create a connection between the opening and ending so the video feels like one complete story.

Avoid a generic logo + tagline ending unless it genuinely works for the concept.

Use previous learnings

Continue using everything learned from previous generations:

* Model scorecard
* Model strengths and weaknesses
* Prompt library
* Human realism techniques
* Transition techniques
* Entry/exit frame planning
* Hyperframes learnings
* Model-routing logic

Do not repeat research that has already been completed unless a new model or technique could meaningfully improve this video.

Choose the best model for every shot rather than forcing one model to create the whole video.

Improvement loop

Run:

concept → script → shot plan → generate → assemble → evaluate → improve weak scenes → regenerate → final review

Evaluate the actual finished video for:

* Strength of the opening
* Product clarity
* Walkthrough clarity
* UI quality
* Pacing
* Visual consistency
* Transition quality
* Human realism where applicable
* Creativity
* Strength of the ending
* Overall watchability

If the opening or ending feels generic, iterate on it.

If the product walkthrough is confusing, simplify it.

If an individual scene looks weaker than the rest of the video, regenerate or replace it.

Budget

Do not exceed the limit.

Use existing learnings to avoid unnecessary model tests and spend the budget primarily on producing and improving the new video.
