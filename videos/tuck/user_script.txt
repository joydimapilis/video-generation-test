But for this version, use minimal human appearance.

Human presence

A person can appear, but only in a subtle and natural way.

The person should support the story without becoming the main subject.

Examples:

* A hand opening a laptop
* A hand using a trackpad or mouse
* Someone sitting at a desk, shown from behind
* A person walking into a workspace
* A shoulder or partial silhouette in the foreground
* Hands interacting with a phone or device
* A quick over-the-shoulder product view
* Someone closing a laptop after completing a task
* A person briefly reacting to a result on screen
* A wide shot where the person is part of the environment

Avoid long close-ups of faces.

Avoid dialogue-heavy scenes.

Avoid direct-to-camera speaking.

Avoid making the video feel like a testimonial or UGC ad.

The person should feel like a real user naturally interacting with the product.

Creative concept

Build the video around a simple transformation:

before the product → interaction with the product → better outcome

For example, show a person starting with a messy or frustrating workflow, then transition into the product experience.

The product should gradually take over visually.

The human presence can become less visible as the video moves deeper into the software walkthrough.

This can create a visual progression like:

real-world problem → product interaction → UI walkthrough → outcome → final product moment

Opening

Create a strong opening using a subtle human moment rather than a talking person.

For example:

* A cursor hovering over too many tabs
* A hand rapidly switching between tools
* Someone sitting in front of a cluttered screen
* A phone notification interrupting a workflow
* A person reaching for a notebook, then stopping
* A close-up of hands dealing with a repetitive task

Use that moment to establish the problem quickly.

Then transition creatively into the product.

The opening should feel cinematic and intentional, not like generic office stock footage.

Product reveal and walkthrough

Move quickly from the human setup into the product.

Show only the most important parts of the workflow.

Use Hyperframes where appropriate for accurate:

* UI
* Text
* Product screens
* Interface transitions
* Motion graphics

Use video models for:

* Hands
* Devices
* Workspace scenes
* Cinematic product moments
* Real-world transitions

The walkthrough should remain short and easy to follow.

Focus on one clear workflow rather than too many features.

Make the product central

The person should not distract from the product.

The viewer should clearly understand:

* What the product is
* What problem it solves
* What action the user takes
* What happens as a result

Keep the product visible and central throughout the video.

Transitions

Use the person and physical environment to create transitions into the UI.

Examples:

* Camera pushes into the laptop screen
* Phone screen expands into a full-frame interface
* Cursor movement continues across the next scene
* A hand gesture triggers the next UI sequence
* A physical object matches the shape or movement of a UI element
* A screen reflection transitions into the product interface

Plan entry and exit frames carefully so the real-world and digital scenes feel connected.

Ending

Bring the human presence back subtly at the end.

For example:

* The task is complete and the person closes the laptop
* A clean result appears, then cut to a relaxed workspace
* The person gets up and leaves while the product remains visible
* A final over-the-shoulder product screen
* A phone is placed down after the workflow is completed

Then finish with a strong product visual or concise CTA.

Try to create a visual callback to the opening so the video feels complete.
