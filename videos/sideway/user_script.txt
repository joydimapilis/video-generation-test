Important: generate the person as an image first

For every important scene involving the main person, follow the same workflow we used in the previous video:

Generate a realistic still image of the person first, then use that image as the reference/start frame for image-to-video generation.

Do not generate the main human scenes directly from text-to-video.

The workflow should be:

1. Generate the person’s source/reference image first.
2. Check that the image looks realistic and that the pose, hands, clothing, lighting, and interaction are correct.
3. If the image looks wrong or obviously AI-generated, regenerate the image before proceeding.
4. Use the approved image for image-to-video generation.
5. Keep movement natural and controlled.

If the same person appears across several shots, keep using the same approved character reference so their identity remains consistent.

Do not rely on the video model to fix an incorrect source image.

Concept direction

Choose the mobile app concept yourself based on what will work best visually and narratively.

It could be something like:

* Productivity

* Communication

* Another mobile-first product that fits the styles in our video library

The concept should be easy for the viewer to understand quickly.

Story structure

Build the video around the person’s experience with the product, rather than a traditional interview.

A possible structure:

experience/problem → app discovery/use → glimpse of app workflow → result → short reflection

The person might say one short line about what they struggled with.

Then move away from the talking shot and visually show:

* Them using their phone
* The app interface
* The relevant feature
* How it fits into their day
* The result or improvement

Later, briefly return to the person for another short line or reaction.

Do not keep the camera on the person speaking for long periods.

Human realism

Before animating any human scene, make sure the source image has:

* Natural facial proportions
* Realistic skin texture and imperfections
* Natural hair
* Correct hands and fingers
* Realistic clothing texture
* Natural lighting
* Appropriate posture
* No obvious AI artifacts
* No overly polished or plastic-looking face

For the image-to-video stage, use subtle motion:

* Natural blinking
* Small eye movements
* Subtle facial expressions
* Slight head movement
* Natural breathing
* Small posture shifts
* Controlled hand movement
* Natural hair and clothing movement

Avoid exaggerated body or facial movement.

Phone interaction

Phone interaction needs to look believable.

Generate the interaction correctly in the source image first.

For example:

* The phone should actually be visible.
* The person’s hand should be positioned naturally around it.
* Fingers should be in the correct position for tapping or scrolling.
* Their gaze should match the phone or environment.
* Avoid distorted device shapes or fingers.

Only animate the scene after the still image looks correct.

For animation, keep the interaction simple:

* Small thumb movement
* Short tap
* Natural scrolling
* Slight hand movement
* Realistic gaze change

App UI

Use Hyperframes or the strongest available UI method for the actual mobile interface.

Do not rely on a general video model to generate important readable UI.

Show only the most important parts of the app.

The viewer should understand:

* What the app does
* What the user is doing
* How it helps
* What result they get

Make the product clear. Do not make the app mention too subtle.

Opening

Do not start with a generic interview shot.

Create a stronger opening using:

* A real-life moment
* A phone interaction
* A notification
* A problem or frustration
* A short visual hook
* Another idea inspired by the video library

Then naturally introduce the person’s experience and the app.

Blend the person and product

Avoid making the video feel like:

interview → app demo → interview

Connect the scenes visually.

For example:

* Person looks at phone → transition into UI
* Finger taps → UI animation continues the movement
* App completes an action → return to the person’s reaction
* Phone screen → full-screen product interface

Think carefully about the entry and exit frames.

Ending

Create a proper ending rather than simply ending on the final spoken line.

The ending can combine:

* The person’s result
* One final app interaction
* A visual callback to the opening
* A strong app screen
* A concise final message or CTA

The opening and ending should feel connected.

Think:
