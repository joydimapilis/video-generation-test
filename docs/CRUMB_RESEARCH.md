## Prompt repositories evaluated

Started at the requested [GitHub video-prompts topic](https://github.com/topics/video-prompts). Rechecked two relevant repositories. No third-party execution code or skill package was installed.

- [Reviral-ai/awesome-kling-prompts](https://github.com/Reviral-ai/awesome-kling-prompts): useful distinction between fixed object properties and one motivated camera or subject movement; specify a physical ending. Applied to the new pastry reveal and shared exit-frame continuation. We test those techniques rather than adopting a complete prompt collection. Product shape and actual joint must still be reviewed.
- [jnMetaCode/ai-shortfilm-prompts](https://github.com/jnMetaCode/ai-shortfilm-prompts): structured scene planning and concrete endings help clarify a shot. Its long cinematic recipes and subjective quality claims are not evidence of better people. The Crumb revision removes dense texture and negative-expression instructions after the first takes overinterpreted them. Retain structure as an optional checklist, not mandatory prompt length. Original scripts remain locally authored; do not copy the repository's separately licensed archival prompts.

## Iteration hypothesis

First-round baker prompts named pores/lines and multiple prohibited expressions. Sampled H3 Max and Veo faces appeared overtextured, and acting stayed emphatic. Second round removes that texture list, uses soft broad daylight, and directs a single communicative intention: calmly explain tomorrow's plan. Same seed per endpoint and same dialogue, but text-only changes regenerate identity and scene, so this is a practical prompt revision, not a controlled causal study.

The product continuation begins at the actual 4.5s decoded pastry frame. Identical start/end references constrain the settled shot. Compare the joint and intermediate frames, not only input stills. This tests a near-static product hold, not moving-character continuity. Do not extrapolate it to walking people or changing camera angles.
