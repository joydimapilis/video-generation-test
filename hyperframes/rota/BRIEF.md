# The Sunday Problem: a customer testimonial for ROTA

One original ~42-second 16:9 customer testimonial, delivered as H.264/AAC MP4.
The user delegates every technical decision and reviews only the final video.
Ledger: `artifacts/library-loop-5/budget.json`, hard $10 estimated cap.

ROTA is a fictional product, Second Shift Bakery is a fictional business, and the
speaker is a fictional person. No real product, company, customer or metric is
depicted, and a footer says so for the whole runtime.

## Why this use case
Interview is the one use case on the shortlist with measured plates and no
delivered film. The first loop scored `interview_veo_v1` at 8.5/10 and wrote down
an open caution - speaker identity across a longer exchange remains untested -
which four films later was still open. This film closes it.

The library's commercial form of that genre is the Chowdeck case study: one
recurring speaker in a distinctive setting, burned-in transcript captions on
every spoken frame, a persistent brand mark, and product b-roll between the
lines. That is the shape this film takes.

## The story
Mara runs a small bakery. Every Sunday night she used to rebuild the week's staff
rota by hand in pencil, and every week somebody's plans changed and she started
again. Now it takes about nine minutes. She got her Sundays back. The film is
careful to keep the claim that small - a modest, specific benefit is what makes a
testimonial credible, and overselling is what makes it worthless.

## Craft targets
- A single speaker sustained across four separate shots, which is the thing the
  first loop could not verify.
- Burned-in transcript captions on every spoken frame, Chowdeck-style, authored
  rather than generated - generated text has failed in all four previous loops.
- A light close card. All four previous films closed on near-black; this one
  closes on cream, because the film is warm and ending dark would fight it.
- Over-coverage again: five b-roll and dialogue plates bought beyond the minimum.

## Audio
No synthesized narration in this film - the voice is the generated performance
itself, which is the point of an interview. A quiet score sits underneath and
ducks for every spoken line. That is the inverse of the previous two films, where
narration was authored and the footage was silent.

## Acceptance
Full MP4 decode; 1080p; every spoken line verified against the written script by
local transcript; captions match the spoken words exactly; per-channel peak
measurement, never a mono downmix; measurement windows derived from the measured
speech schedule rather than hardcoded; no overflow or overlapping text; contrast
passes on every caption and card.
