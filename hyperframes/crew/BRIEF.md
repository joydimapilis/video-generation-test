# The Sunday Problem (revised): a customer testimonial for Crew

A revision of `artifacts/final_outcome/rota/rota-final.mp4`. Same woman, same
bakery, same testimonial form - but the product and its value are now explicit.
~44s, 16:9, H.264/AAC. Ledger `artifacts/library-loop-6/budget.json`, $10 cap.

Crew is a fictional product, Second Shift Bakery is a fictional business and Mara
Osman is a fictional person. A footer says so for the whole runtime.

## What changed and why
The first cut told the story but never said what she actually uses or what it
does. "Nine minutes" had no stated subject. And the product was called ROTA -
British trade jargon for a staff schedule, which is exactly the word a viewer
outside the UK would stumble on.

So: the product is renamed **Crew**, the script says **staff schedule** in plain
words, and two new lines carry the mechanic - what she uses, and how it works.
The arc is now explicit: problem, then what she uses, then how it helps, then the
result.

| beat | line |
| --- | --- |
| setup | We're a bakery. Six people, two ovens, and a lot of very early mornings. |
| problem | Every Sunday night I'd sit down with a pencil and rebuild the staff schedule. |
| the pain | Someone's got an exam. Someone's kid is sick. Start again. |
| what she uses | We use Crew now. Everyone puts their availability on their phone. |
| how it helps | It builds the schedule around that, and everyone sees it update. |
| the result | Now the whole schedule takes about nine minutes. I got my Sundays back. |

## Staying customer-focused
She describes her working week; she never recommends anything. There is no
superlative in the script, no statistic beyond her own "about nine minutes", and
the two product lines are mechanical rather than enthusiastic - "everyone puts
their availability on their phone" is how a person talks, not how an ad talks.
The claim stays deliberately small, because a modest specific benefit is what
makes a testimonial credible.

## Revision economics
One of the four original dialogue takes - line 1 - says nothing that changed, so
it is reused verbatim along with all four b-roll plates; line 3 was re-generated
after an offensive hand gesture was found in the original take. Only the four lines whose words
changed were re-bought - $4.20 rather than a whole new film. Every new line was
generated from the same reference frame the originals came from, so it is
provably the same woman throughout.

## What carries the product clarity
Two authored product screens, not generated ones. Five loops have established
that no generative model spells reliably, and the screens here have to show real
names, real days and a real schedule filling in. They are also the only place the
film explains the mechanic visually, so they must be exactly right.

## Acceptance
Full MP4 decode; 1080p; every spoken line verified against the written script by
local transcript; captions match the spoken words; captions legible over both
bright bakery footage and the dark product screens; per-channel peak measurement;
measurement windows derived from the measured speech schedule; contrast passes.
